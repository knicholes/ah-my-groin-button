# Case v2 — 120 × 120 × 140 mm

Generated by `case/build_v2.py`. Do not edit anything in this folder by hand;
re-run the script instead. This supersedes `case/v1-200x130x110`, which laid
everything out side by side on one tray and was consequently very wide.

## Print these

| File | Size | Copies | Orientation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `body.stl` | 120.00 × 120.00 × 140.00 mm | 1 | Top panel **down** on the plate. Printed the other way up the top panel is a ~113 mm unsupported span. Expect support at the four corner blocks and the lip. |
| `bottom.stl` | 102.20 × 102.20 × 57.00 mm | 1 | As modelled — flat underside on the plate, posts and cradle growing up. The battery cradle floor at z=39 is a bridge off its support wall and will want support under it. |
| `foot.stl` | 14.00 × 14.00 × 8.00 mm | **4** | Flat side down, countersink facing **up**. No support. Flipped at assembly so the screw head ends up on the floor. |

0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layer, 20 % infill, PLA or PETG. Four M3 brass heat-set
inserts in the body's corner blocks; screws are M3 × 25 mm.

**Check the size your slicer reports.** These are millimetres. The build reads
each STL back after writing it and fails if the bounding box is wrong, but a
slicer will load a mis-scaled file without complaint, so look anyway.

## How it stacks

## The three constraints that shaped this

* **The PCB cannot sit on posts rising straight from the tray.** Its mount
  holes span 80 mm, the speaker is 77.8 mm wide, and 6 mm posts need 86 mm to
  straddle it. Short by 3.8 mm — and that is independent of box size, so
  widening the case does not fix it. Four posts rise in the clear bands beside
  the speaker and reach inward with arms at z=37, above the speaker's top face
  at z=35. If you change the speaker or the board, re-check that 2 mm.
* **Anything mounted to the tray has to pass up through the 103 mm bottom
  opening.** The tray is lifted into the shell as one loaded column. The
  clearance checks enforce this; it is easy to violate by nudging the battery
  outward toward its wall.
* **The top rim round is what limits the button.** At r=6 the flat top is
  108 mm across and the 99 mm flange has 4.5 mm of seating. Rounding the top
  edge further starts to undercut the flange.

## Assumption to verify

`BTN_SWITCH_D = 40.0` — the microswitch's width was never measured, so the
model reserves a deliberately oversized 40 mm envelope for it. A real one is
more like 27 mm. It only has to clear the PCB below it and there is 5.8 mm of
margin, so a smaller true value only adds slack. Worth a caliper check.
